# Template: data note

One finding that only joined sources show, for economists and economics students. Written by the data desk from
datasteg's own apps, on a day something gives it a reason: new data, an economy at an extreme of its own
past, an event, or a paper received that month.

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 650–1,100 English words (4–6 minutes) |
| Figures | 3–4 charts in at least two forms (at most one scatter, a line for any history); one shows the join itself |
| Sources | at least two apps and two publishers; every number a computed fact |
| Approval | the editor approves the finished note in the admin site; nothing is published before |

## Shape

1. **Header.** The title is the finding (at most 90 characters, no question, no colon). The dek gives who, how many,
   which period and which sources in one or two sentences. "Why this note" (`whyNow`): what is new today and why
   these economies, in one or two sentences.
2. **Key numbers.** Three: the finding's number, its base, and its scale.
3. **Findings.** Three or four sentences a reader could quote, each with its number and period.
4. **The question box.** `asked`: the question in a reader's words. `lookedAt`: the sources joined and the period of
   each, in one or two sentences. `premiseNote`: empty unless the data overturn a common belief.
5. **Sections**, four or five, each opening with its point:
   - *the finding*: the joined number, the named economies, the figure that shows the join;
   - *each side alone*: what the funding source measures and shows, what the trade source measures and shows, in
     plain words, before they are joined;
   - *why it would matter*: the mechanism, as conditions ("if … then …"), with the scale;
   - *what this cannot tell us* (`id: limits`): periods that differ, coverage, what a score counts, the rival
     reading the numbers cannot rule out;
   - *how we counted* (`id: method`): two or three sentences on the join, and where the reader can open the data.
6. **Our read** (the insight box): one paragraph of judgement, marked as ours: what a reader should watch next and
   what would change the finding. No forecast.

Never: a section of background the finding does not need; a closing summary; a number without its period.
